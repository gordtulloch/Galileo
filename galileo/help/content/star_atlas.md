# Star Atlas

The Star Atlas is a planetarium: a live map of the sky as seen from your observatory, with stars, deep-sky objects, the Sun, Moon and planets, and the position of each telescope. Use it to see what is up, find an object, and send your mount to it.

**Left:** the time, site and display options. **Centre:** the sky map. **Right:** details of the object you have selected, with buttons to act on it.

**On the map:** click an object to identify it; double-click to centre and track it; drag to pan; scroll to zoom. Right-click an object to *Goto*, *Sync* or *Add to Session*, or right-click empty sky to Goto that point.

The site follows the Observatory selected in the top bar; edit it there for a permanent change.

## Sky View

The controls on the left that change what the map shows and when.

## Sky map

The sky as seen from the site at the chosen time. Click to select, double-click to centre and track, drag to pan, scroll to zoom. Right-click for the Goto / Sync / Add to Session menu.

## Find

Type the name of a star, planet or deep-sky object (for example Vega, Jupiter or M31) and press Enter. The map centres on it and selects it. If nothing matches, the status bar says so; if it is below the horizon at the chosen time and place, it tells you that too.

## Time (Local)

The date and time shown, in your local time. Editing it turns **Live** off so the map stays where you put it.

## Step time

Moves the time by minus one day, minus one hour, plus one hour or plus one day. Handy for watching an object rise or for checking tomorrow night.

## Live (follow the clock)

When ticked, the map follows the computer's clock and moves with the sky. Untick it (or edit the time) to freeze on a particular moment.

## Latitude

The latitude of the viewing site, in degrees (north positive). It comes from the Observatory selected in the top bar, but you can look from anywhere by editing it here.

## Longitude

The longitude of the viewing site, in degrees (east positive).

## Star magnitude limit

How faint the stars drawn can be. Lower numbers show only the brightest stars; higher show more. 6 is roughly what the unaided eye sees from a dark site.

## Deep-sky magnitude limit

How faint the deep-sky objects (galaxies, nebulae, clusters) drawn can be.

## Coordinate grid

Draws lines of right ascension and declination.

## Constellation boundaries

Draws the official borders between constellations.

## Constellation outlines

Draws the stick-figure patterns that join the main stars of each constellation.

## Abbreviate constellation names

Uses the standard three-letter abbreviations (Ori, UMa…) instead of full names, to reduce clutter.

## Deep-sky objects

Shows galaxies, nebulae and star clusters from the catalogs listed below.

## Sun, Moon & planets

Shows the Sun, the Moon (with its phase) and the planets.

## Labels

Shows names beside objects on the map.

## Ground

Shades the sky below the horizon.

## Daylight sky

Brightens the sky background when the Sun is up (or twilight), as the real sky would look.

## Horizon

Draws the horizon line, including the horizon profile configured for the Observatory in Options › Star Atlas.

## Telescope markers

Draws a marker for where each Pier's telescope is pointing right now, so you can see where your mounts are looking.

## Catalogs

Which deep-sky catalogs are drawn: Messier, Caldwell and NGC, with the number of objects in each. Press **+** to add one, or **×** beside a catalog to remove it.

## Add catalog

Choose another deep-sky catalog to draw on the map.

## Remove catalog

Stops drawing this catalog's objects on the map.

## Name

The selected object's name, with its catalog designation if it has one.

## Type

What kind of object it is: star, galaxy, nebula, cluster, planet and so on.

## Magnitude

How bright it appears. Smaller numbers are brighter; negative is brighter than a zero-magnitude star.

## Constellation

The constellation it lies in.

## RA (J2000)

Its right ascension on the J2000 sky grid.

## Dec (J2000)

Its declination on the J2000 sky grid.

## Altitude

How high it is above the horizon at the time shown, in degrees. Negative means below the horizon.

## Azimuth

Its compass direction at the time shown, in degrees from north through east.

## Goto

Slews the current Pier's mount to the selected object. The mount must be connected and unparked, and the target above any limits set on the Mount page.

## Sync

Tells the current Pier's mount that it is pointing at the selected object. Use it after centring the object by eye or plate solve, to correct the mount's pointing model.

## Add to Session

Creates a new session on Planning › Sessions that already contains a Target block for the selected object.
